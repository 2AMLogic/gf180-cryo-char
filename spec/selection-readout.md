# Selection, readout and leakage budget

Decision 0001 proposes four force/sense pairs and one guard, shared by the structures in the [structure map](structure-map.md). No timing or leakage result for the complete selection network exists yet.

## Address and switching

Use ADDR[8:0] as the stable map code. ADDR[9] selects the physical replica for precision resistors and rings. Set MEAS_EN low, stop the oscillator, set the address, then assert MEAS_EN with the address stable before a rising clock edge. The decoder registers the address, opens every previous branch, waits one full selection-clock cycle, then enables one bank and one DUT and raises READY. Hold the address and stop CLK during a sensitive measurement. RST_N and deasserting MEAS_EN disconnect the buses and disable all rings. Reserved/dropped/cut codes and an unsupported replica selection stay disconnected with READY low.

Low-bank code 0 opens every branch. Codes 510 and 511 select open and short calibration structures with matched wiring. Code 510 ends in an unconnected DUT-side pad replica; code 511 joins the DUT-side force and sense contacts on the two Kelvin terminals with a short metal strap. Codes 494–509 are reserved. Neither calibration reference counts as a NIST DUT.

| Class | Terminals presented to the external instrument | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| Array and isolated FET | Pair 1 D, 2 G, 3 S, 4 B. The isolated module's fifth source label is X. Its function is unspecified in the workbook and slide 7; leave it unconnected unless a source-backed revision assigns it. | Sweep gate or drain, acquire currents and actual DUT terminal voltages. For a substrate-tied device, hold body at VSS and vary source only within legal bias. Never force the body against the substrate. |
| MOS and overlap capacitors | Pair 1 gate, 2 common S/D, 3 body when separate. Where specified, short S/D/B at the DUT as on the source module. | Guarded small-signal C–V about a programmed DC bias. |
| Diodes and MIM | Pair 1 anode/top, 2 cathode/bottom, with well or substrate ties held at their permitted potentials. | Reverse-biased I–V and C–V for diodes, C–V for the MIM structures. Observe voltage polarity and terminal limits. |
| Precision resistors, metal and via chains | F1/S1 at the top end and F2/S2 at the bottom end. Independent sense contacts inside the force connections. | Determine R from sensed voltage difference and measured force current, reverse current to check offsets. |
| Ring oscillators | Pair 1 forces/senses VRO, pair 2 returns/senses local ground. RO_EN enables only the selected ring while MEAS_EN is active. | Count RO_OUT over a stated gate interval. Multiply by 1,024 to obtain the ring frequency. Record VRO and supply current. Require a valid digital crossing between VRO and the divider/readout rail before claiming a point. |

## Using the shared guard

The single externally driven GUARD on analog[8] supplies one shield potential. For a low-current drain measurement, drive it to the measured drain potential and hold the gate, source and body at their required biases. To measure another terminal's smallest current, repeat with the guard referenced to that terminal. Record every simultaneous force and sense voltage. Only terminals tracking the same guard voltage and waveform can share its suppression of leakage. For simultaneous unequal biases, qualify each terminal under that exact configuration before assigning it a range.

With two terminals at 0 and 3.3 V, any common guard differs from at least one by 1.65 V or more. A guard tracking the first terminal leaves 3.3 V across the second terminal's guard insulation. In a linear leakage path, keeping this contribution alone at 5 fA at 3.3 V requires at least 660 TΩ. At 10 kHz, a 10 mV RMS difference couples 2.2 pA through 3.50 fF, consuming the entire area-diode C–V allocation. These are sensitivities to an actual bias difference. Use extracted coupling and physical pad, fixture and open measurements to determine the contribution. The pad protection junctions, inactive MOS branches and local wells have their own biases.

For every proposed precision configuration record which terminal the guard tracks, driver compliance, settling, DC error and AC amplitude/phase difference. Repeat the matched-open and standard with the intended guard drive, then with a second legal guard setting at a known voltage or AC phase difference. Sweep other pairs over the intended bias range while observing the selected reading. Carry observed cross-coupling and guard drift into the floor and uncertainty. Maintain substrate and well guard-ring ties independently of this driven measurement shield. Publish any unqualified simultaneous measurement as unresolved.

## Numerical acceptance

The smallest nonzero FET and diode DC readings in the NIST archive are 10 fA. This is the reference instrument's recorded increment near zero. Use 5 fA as the design target for the magnitude of the total signed switch current delivered to one selected terminal by all branches together over the predeclared supported bias conditions. The 5 fA allocation is half the 10 fA recording step. It is a switch design target; measurement confidence comes from independent physical controls. The pre-freeze design screen includes all five MOS process corners at −40, 25 and 125 °C. For every affected path also sweep the supplied typical/slow/fast diode, MOS-capacitor, MIM-capacitor and resistor model sections as applicable. Record model-section combinations, rail and terminal bias, inactive terminal states, and temperature. Use the adverse combination for each quantity and document any correlation imposed by the PDK. Use the published worst value over that grid and report the temperature, voltage and bias envelope of any narrower physical claim. Cryogenic sensitivity requires measurements at the claimed temperature. The GF180 measurement range comes from the physical controls below. Isolated FETs use the same qualification because the archive has no separate measured I–V class for that worksheet.

For AC the archive reports capacitance. The equivalent current below uses the proposed GF test conditions of 10 mV RMS at 10 kHz and the relation 2πfVC. The 1 µA oscillator supply signal is a design assumption. The table covers raw aggregate mux loading at one selected terminal. The four AC and three resistance allocations take at most one tenth of the stated reference current, rounded down at the precision shown. For RO, 100 nA is one tenth of the assumed supply signal. Characterize the pad and fixture background separately at the same operating point.

## AC, resistance and oscillator current qualification

For C–V, apply 10 mV RMS at 10 kHz with the declared DC bias and terminate every inactive branch as it will be used. Measure the complex terminal current on independently matched direct and multiplexed standards of known admittance. Match the pad, secondary protection, fixture and standards and bound their mismatch and drift. The raw mux contribution is the magnitude of the complex difference, including both conductance and susceptance. Its upper 95% bound must meet the class allocation. Perform open/short compensation using 510/511 and report the calibrated DUT error against a separate standard with its uncertainty. For a resolved capacitance, the upper 95% bound on a separately validated, corrected open must be below the smallest claimed capacitance and the DUT result, and the DUT interval must exclude zero. Require calibrated recovery of a separate capacitance standard at that endpoint and state a separate uncertainty target for precision claims. A small residual cannot erase a raw-limit miss. Publish both the extracted prediction and the physical result or its pending status.

For Kelvin resistance, use independently characterized standards spanning the intended range. Reverse the forced current and measure both polarities through direct and muxed paths. At equal sensed DUT voltage, bound the absolute difference of the force currents including measurement uncertainty against the class allocation. Report the four-wire resistance and uncertainty from the sensed voltage and current separately, including reversal offsets and settling. For the ring rail compare direct and selected supply currents at equal sensed VRO, activity and load, with the timing and uncertainty of both readings recorded. Size the ring force switch using the observed peak and average running currents across voltage, load and process. Record the voltage at the supply pad and the sensed VRO, including startup and capture. The 1 µA allocation reference is independent of the required drive capability. Record every exceeded allocation by code, condition and sign. A change to any bound or its raw-versus-corrected meaning requires a new decision.

With a typical 25 °C grounded far terminal, one open reference switch draws 1.625 pA (2.586 fF), using 74% of the area-diode limit. Four such branches draw 6.500 pA. That test topology exceeds the area-diode, area-MOS and MIM limits. The full bank requires extraction and a physical standard of its own. [90-point AC control](../sim/switch-loading/ac-loading.csv)

## Population and mismatch

The 10-bit decoder addresses 64 banks of 16 members. The proposed map has 372 primary devices, 12 additional replica addresses and two calibration paths, giving 386 populated addresses in 29 banks. A shared terminal connected to every populated bank sees up to 28 off-bank isolation switches and up to 15 idle member switches in a selected full bank, plus any parallel force/sense or protection paths. A flat single-branch-per-address test would retain 385 off paths with one selected. Record the actual counts for each terminal and all-open reset; a sparse address map alone cannot bound its capacitance. Enumerate every physically connected off branch, calibration branch, replica, and bank isolation switch visible from each force or sense terminal for each enabled selection and reset. Tabulate the maximum population with the corresponding code, routing tree and inactive biases. Use the full extracted tree including the capacitances of isolated sub-banks. The single-switch and four-parallel-switch controls do not supply a per-branch design allocation or an assumed bank size. Until the routed tree gives that population, the whole-bank result is pending.

The [seeded four-branch control](../sim/switch-loading/README.md#local-mismatch-of-four-open-branches) measures 128 local-mismatch realizations at each of three conditions. At FF/125 °C, the one-sided upper bounds on the 95th percentile of absolute DC loading are 191.86 pA at 0 V measured / 3.3 V far and 195.30 pA with the bias reversed. These schematic controls miss both the 5 fA design target and the reference-scale raw loading requirement. The complete routed tree and physical measurement remain pending.

T4 has a statistical component for the signed aggregate loading. In addition to deterministic corners, run mismatch Monte Carlo on the extracted switches at the adverse leakage and admittance conditions with the pinned PDK's documented mismatch controls enabled. Preserve the random seeds, run count, correlations and one-sided confidence bound on the 95th percentile of absolute loading. Report that statistic alongside the worst deterministic result and both polarities. Document missing mismatch models by device. Physical pad and bank mismatch, drift, blank floor and recovery still require independent measurements. T1–T3 and T5 contain no statistical matching or yield specification.

## FET and diode DC qualification

1. **Declare the claim before collecting DUT data.** Record the terminal and structure codes, smallest claimed absolute current `I_min`, sign, terminal and inactive-branch bias ranges, temperature, bank population, guard setting, instrument range, settling time, integration time and averaging. Evaluate each configuration that the claim covers. Retain the original range and result if a narrower range is later proposed.
2. **Measure the floor.** Record repeated instrument/fixture blanks with the guarded input terminated as it will be probed. Separately characterize the powered pad and its actual secondary protection on a pad-only structure or a demonstrably equivalent coupon. Sweep pad and rail bias, temperature and time. Record the signed raw current, its change between matched pads and, for AC, the complex open admittance. Repeat through the biased pad and matched on-die open (code 510) with the same bank loading as the DUT. Attribute the difference to the selected route only within the measured mismatch and drift uncertainty. Use the short (511) to check continuity and settling. Bracket the DUT series with blanks to measure drift. Use separate blanks to fit and to validate any offset correction. Report raw signed currents and the upper 95% prediction bound on the absolute corrected matched-blank reading, `F95`, for one result using the declared acquisition procedure. Document the repeat count, time span and method supporting that coverage, including drift or correlated samples.
3. **Verify current recovery and uncertainty.** Apply an independently calibrated current at the DUT side, or through a demonstrated equivalent replica of the complete selected path. Exercise the smallest proposed current and each claimed polarity. Include the reference source, repeatability, quantization, offset correction, pad mismatch, drift and residual mux error in the uncertainty budget once each, with correlations where applicable. Report expanded uncertainty `U95`, its coverage factor and its basis. The recovered current must have the expected sign and agree with the calibrated input within their combined 95% uncertainty. An external pad injection that bypasses the switches qualifies only the fixture and pad. [NIST guidance on expanded uncertainty](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-6-expanded-uncertainty)
4. **Decide the range and report each point.** To qualify the declared range, require `F95 < I_min` and an upper bound on worst-case absolute raw aggregate mux loading below `I_min`. At the smallest injected current, and for each DUT reading reported as resolved, require `abs(I_corrected) > F95` and a 95% current interval that excludes zero. Report the separate raw pad/clamp and switch bounds at the same operating point and the total raw current before correction. Carry any pad-background subtraction, mismatch and drift into F95 and U95 using independent physical blanks. A model-only subtraction or an unmeasured pad/protection background leaves the range pending. A precision claim must also meet its stated absolute or relative uncertainty. Record readings below the qualified range as unresolved with the measured floor and uncertainty. Record a 5 fA design-target miss independently, even when a larger current range qualifies.

The reference-scale detection claim has a fixed smallest test current of **10 fA** in each claimed polarity. To claim it, report the physical tests above at 10 fA. If these fail or are unavailable, publish that claim as failed or pending and retain the original 5 fA raw target and its outcome. A laboratory may characterize a larger predeclared range and publish the actual numerical lower endpoint, raw-loading bound, floor and uncertainty. Counting that range in place of the reference-scale commitment for a release or a Bronze grant requires a separate numeric spec decision, independent review and operator approval.

At the December freeze, publish the extracted mux bounds, the raw controls, and the status of each proposed range. The 107.45 pA modeled hot-rail pad background precludes an uncorrected 10 fA claim at that point. Independent matched blanks would have to bound its correction and drift to below the endpoint, including pad-to-pad differences. Publish any qualifying subset by temperature, bias and pad. A physical floor established on a representative coupon must identify how its pad, switch path, loading and fixture match. If physical controls are unavailable, label the range **pending measurement**. Simulations support design review and cannot establish the pad-and-fixture floor or qualify a cryogenic temperature.

## What must be measured before integration

At 25 °C, typical corner, a single unguarded open transmission gate (n/p 3.3 V devices, W=L=1 µm, finite junction area on both sides) delivers **68.20 fA** into a 0 V ideal instrument with its far terminal at 3.3 V in ngspice 47. With its far terminal also at 0 V it still delivers **63.53 fA**. Both absolute values miss the 5 fA FET/diode design target before other branches and the pad enter the circuit. Their difference is the change with far-terminal bias. The 63.53 fA baseline remains in the terminal current. At equal 0 V terminals, the pinned BSIM model reports 59.81 fA PMOS gate-induced source leakage (IGISL) and a 3.73 fA magnitude source/body junction contribution. IGISL is the source-side counterpart of gate-induced drain leakage. The two internal ends of this symmetric device each report a gate-induced term. Use only the term associated with the measured terminal in its current balance.

Holding the PMOS well at 3.3 V and reducing its gate from 3.3 V to 1.65 V at equal 0 V terminals lowers total loading from 63.53 to 3.73 fA in this typical, 25 °C simulation. The same fixed gate bias with the far terminal at 3.3 V delivers **10.60 µA** into the instrument, as the high terminal turns on the PMOS channel. Intermediate gate bias requires tracking of both switch terminals, with off-state and transition checks across the claimed range. At equal zero terminals and 1.65 V gate bias, ten of fifteen modeled process/temperature points meet 5 fA. The worst is 160.77 fA. At the normal 3.3 V gate bias all fifteen miss at either far-terminal voltage. At FF, 125 °C and a 3.3 V far terminal, the worst raw loading is **42.28 pA**. The BSIM NMOS drain current is 39.30 pA and PMOS drain current 2.71 pA. The cold/equal-terminal gate-induced explanation cannot be carried over to this hot, unequal-terminal state. These are single-switch negative controls. [120-point bias and PVT record](../sim/switch-loading/README.md)

Room-temperature model data cannot establish behaviour at liquid-helium temperatures.
